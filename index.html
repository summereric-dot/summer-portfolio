😊About

I am a researcher working at the intersection of biomaterials, nanomedicine, and functional material design. My academic interests centre on developing advanced material systems for biomedical applications, with particular attention to targeted delivery, therapeutic performance, and structure–function relationships.

This portfolio presents my research interests, selected projects, and technical skills.

My work is motivated by the idea that well-designed materials can address practical clinical and biological challenges more effectively than conventional approaches. I am especially interested in how material composition, morphology, surface chemistry, and supramolecular organisation influence biological interactions and application outcomes.

Across my research, I aim to combine scientific rigour with translational relevance. This includes designing materials with clear functional purpose, evaluating their physicochemical behaviour, and considering how they may be adapted for realistic biomedical or therapeutic use.

In addition to research, I value scientific communication, structured problem-solving, and interdisciplinary collaboration. I am interested in projects that connect material science, biomedical engineering, and practical health-related innovation.

🧪Research

Biomaterials for Biomedical Applications

My research explores the design and evaluation of functional biomaterials for biomedical use. I am interested in how material architecture, chemical composition, and surface properties can be tuned to achieve specific therapeutic or biological functions. This includes studying material stability, responsiveness, compatibility, and application-driven performance.

Nanomedicine and Targeted Delivery Systems

A major focus of my work is the development of nanostructured delivery systems for improved therapeutic precision. I am particularly interested in targeted platforms that can enhance localisation, control release behaviour, and reduce undesirable off-target effects. This area combines material engineering with biological insight to improve treatment efficiency and translational potential.

Supramolecular and Functional Material Systems

I also investigate supramolecular and structurally adaptive material systems, including assemblies formed through non-covalent and coordination-based interactions. These systems are of interest because they offer tunable physical properties, emergent functionality, and opportunities for designing materials with novel processing and application behaviour.

📘Projects

Project 1: Targeted Nanoparticle Systems for Anti-inflammatory Therapy

This project investigates nanoparticle-based systems designed to improve the delivery of anti-inflammatory therapeutics to disease-relevant biological targets. The central aim is to enhance delivery efficiency and local therapeutic effect while reducing non-specific exposure and systemic side effects.

The work involves material design, formulation optimisation, and evaluation of structural and functional properties relevant to biomedical use. Particular emphasis is placed on how nanoscale design features influence performance, including carrier behaviour, delivery selectivity, and therapeutic response.

More broadly, this project contributes to the development of rationally engineered delivery systems for complex inflammatory conditions where conventional treatment strategies may lack precision or sufficient efficacy.

Project 2: Supramolecular Material Platforms Based on Metal–Ligand or Metal–Biomolecule

This project focuses on the preparation and study of supramolecular material systems formed through coordination and non-covalent interactions. The goal is to understand how component selection, composition, and processing conditions affect the structure, stability, and emergent properties of the resulting materials.

A key part of the project is examining structure–property relationships, including thermal behaviour, physical appearance, processability, and functional potential. These studies are relevant to the broader design of adaptive and application-driven material systems.

The project is particularly valuable because it combines fundamental materials chemistry with the possibility of future practical applications in biomedical and advanced functional material contexts.

Project 3: Functional Material Design for Translational Biomedical Use

This project explores how advanced materials can be developed with translational application in mind from the earliest stages of design. Rather than focusing only on novelty, the work considers practicality, reproducibility, performance, and the ability of a material system to address real biomedical needs.

The project integrates material synthesis, property evaluation, and application-based thinking to create systems that are scientifically interesting and potentially useful in therapeutic or diagnostic settings.
